                Recognize the approach, not just the message

                Six patterns worth slowing down for.

                Scams can arrive by text, email, phone, social media, or a QR code. The story changes; pressure, secrecy, and requests for money or access are recurring warning signs.

                01 / LOGIN

                Account and password alerts

                A message says your account will close or has been locked, then sends a sign-in link. Open the service’s official app or type its known address yourself instead.

                02 / DELIVERY

                Parcel and payment fees

                Unexpected delivery charges or failed-payment notices may lead to a fake checkout page. Check tracking and fees directly with the carrier or seller you used.

                03 / MONEY

                Prize, investment, or job offers

                Guaranteed returns, surprise winnings, or easy-income offers can hide advance fees and risky transfers. Verify the organization independently and never pay to unlock a prize.

                04 / IDENTITY

                Urgent calls from someone you know

                A caller claims to be family, a manager, or an official and demands secrecy or immediate payment. Hang up and contact the person using a saved, trusted number.

                05 / ACCESS

                Tech support and remote access

                Unsolicited “support” may ask you to install an app, share your screen, or reveal a code. Do not grant access; contact the company through its official support channel.

                06 / SCAN

                QR codes and payment changes

                A QR code can conceal its destination, and payment details can be altered in a genuine-looking thread. Preview the address and confirm payment changes through a separate trusted channel.

                These examples are warning signs, not proof. ScamCheck checks patterns and available community reports; it cannot verify a sender’s identity or guarantee that a message is safe.

                Protect your phone before it goes missing

                Build your device safety baseline.

                A few settings prepared in advance can make a lost phone easier to secure and your accounts harder to reach.

                01 / LOCK

                Use a strong screen lock

                Set a long passcode or PIN and enable biometrics if you want them. Avoid easy-to-guess codes and keep your recovery method current.

                02 / LOCATE

                Set up the official find service

                Enable your platform’s built-in device-finding and remote-lock tools now, and learn how to reach them from another trusted device.

                03 / PRIVACY

                Limit lock-screen details

                Review notification previews and what can be opened while locked. Keep sensitive messages and account codes off the lock screen.

                04 / RECOVER

                Keep recovery info safe

                Back up important data and store your device serial or IMEI privately somewhere separate from the phone.

                If your phone is lost or stolen

                Secure accounts first. Stay safe.

                Use trusted services and act from another device. The order may vary by your phone, carrier, and location.

                1. Mark it lost and lock itUse the official Apple or Google device-finding service from a trusted device. Avoid links sent by strangers claiming they found your phone.
                2. Secure your key accountsStart with your primary email and financial accounts. Change passwords through official apps or sites and revoke sessions you do not recognize.
                3. Contact your mobile carrierAsk about suspending the SIM or eSIM and protecting your number. Ask whether device blocking is available in your region.
                4. Report it with your recordsContact local authorities as appropriate and provide proof of ownership or the device identifier privately when requested.

                Personal safety comes first: do not confront or follow anyone based on a location estimate. This website cannot remotely locate, lock, ring, or recover a real device.

                Your everyday safety kit

                Stay safe from scams.

                A few simple habits can make it much harder for scammers to rush, trick, or pressure you.

                The golden rule

                Stay sharp.
                Stay safe from scams!

                01 / VERIFY

                Pause & verify

                Contact the person or company using a number or website you found independently—not the details in the message.

                02 / PROTECT

                Keep secrets secret

                Never share passwords, PINs, recovery phrases, or one-time codes—even with someone claiming to be support.

                03 / TAKE A BEAT

                Don’t act under pressure

                Urgency, threats, secrecy, and “too good to be true” rewards are reasons to stop and double-check.
